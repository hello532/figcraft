Free Figma alternatives: three routes, three different bargains

Free is a price, not a category. These three routes cost nothing today and charge you in three different currencies: seats, limits, or maintenance.

What people mean by a free Figma alternative

Three products genuinely cost nothing to start, and each is free for a different reason. Figma Starter is the vendor's own free tier: unlimited drafts, 150 AI credits a day up to 500 a month, and paid seats from the point where a team appears. Penpot Professional is a loss leader with a real workspace attached: eight team members, unlimited viewers, 10GB of storage, seven days of version history, described by the vendor as free and fully featured. FigCraft is free because it is open source under MIT OR Apache-2.0 and there is no company between you and the binary.

None of them is a free copy of Figma. The question worth answering is which bargain matches your work, so the comparison below is organised around what each route refuses to give you.

Route one: Figma Starter

Starter is free and unlimited on drafts, which is more generous than most people expect: a solo designer can work for months without paying. The cap that matters is not document count but collaboration. Team libraries, shared files and the dev handoff loop are what the paid tiers sell, and free viewers can inspect and copy CSS on paid plans, so the free tier is deliberately weakest exactly where a second person appears. If your plan is to learn Figma and you expect to join a team that already pays for it, Starter is the rational route: your files, your shortcuts and your habits all move with you.

Route two: Penpot Professional

This is the strongest free tier for a group. Eight members, unlimited viewers, 10GB and seven days of history, with multiplayer as a first-class feature rather than a preview. Because Penpot is web-first, nobody installs anything, and because it is open source under MPL-2.0 it can be self-hosted; the vendor also sells a Private Server at $50,000 a year for organisations that need that deployment.

The limit is depth of history, not features. Seven days means a mistake discovered next week is outside the window, so the free tier assumes you export backups yourself. Storage at 10GB is comfortable for interface work and not for a library of high-resolution imagery. When you exceed either, the arithmetic is $7 per editor with a monthly ceiling of $175 per team, which is 25 editors exactly.

Route three: FigCraft

FigCraft is free without a funnel, because there is nothing to sell: no account, no telemetry, no hosted service. You download a native binary for macOS, Windows, Linux or FreeBSD, or load the WebAssembly build, and open a .figcraft file from disk. Export covers SVG, PNG, JPG, WebP and PDF, and handoff covers CSS, HTML, React, Leptos, SwiftUI and Compose. Automation runs through a CLI, a JSON control channel and an MCP server with more than 400 commands.

The exclusions do not show up in a feature list. Multiplayer is a preview with no accounts or permissions, and the public server is not end-to-end encrypted. The project calls itself early alpha at roughly 49% parity by its own estimate. Fonts need a sibling ../craft-fonts directory and issue #821 is open on that. macOS 27 on M1 crashes at launch (#781), Windows has a DX12 blur problem, 1440p interface scaling is broken (#540, #818), and FreeBSD aarch64 was dropped in v0.9.0. There is no support contract either.

Four questions that settle it

  1. Will anyone else edit the file this quarter? If yes, Figma Starter or Penpot; FigCraft's multiplayer is a preview and does not replace permissions.
  2. Must the file live on your disk or on an air-gapped machine? Then FigCraft, which needs no account and no network, or a self-hosted Penpot if the team is the unit.
  3. Do you need code or assets out of the tool, repeatedly? FigCraft's CLI and MCP surface is the free answer; Figma gates the equivalent behind Dev seats.
  4. Is your time cheaper than $16 a month? That is the honest Figma question. A Full seat is $16; an afternoon lost to an alpha bug is worth more to most people, and less to anyone who enjoys the bug.

Questions this page answers

Straight answers, with the limits stated. Every claim here is checkable against the sources linked above.

What is the best free Figma alternative?

For a team that needs multiplayer today, Penpot Professional: free for up to 8 members with 10GB and 7 days of history. For a solo designer who wants local files and no account, FigCraft. For someone learning Figma who will later join a paid team, Figma Starter itself, because unlimited drafts are free and the skills transfer.

Is Figma Starter actually free forever?

The draft workspace is free with no stated time limit: unlimited drafts and 150 AI credits a day up to 500 a month. What it withholds is collaboration. Team libraries, permissions and the dev loop sit behind seats, with Full at $16, Dev at $12 and Collab at $3 per month on annual billing.

Does Penpot free really include multiplayer?

Yes. Members and viewers are the unit its pricing is built on: Professional includes 8 team members and unlimited viewers at $0, with 10GB of storage and 7 days of version history. The limits are headcount, storage and history depth, not a disabled multiplayer mode.

What does free FigCraft exclude?

Hosted infrastructure and support. There is no server to sign in to, no account system, no telemetry and no support contract. Multiplayer is a preview without permissions or end-to-end encryption on the public server, and the app is early alpha at about 49% parity by the project own estimate.

Can I move from Figma Starter to FigCraft later?

Partly. FigCraft imports .fig files on a best-effort basis and drops features it cannot represent without a full report; large files can fail (#574, #321). Import to read and reference, keep the original, and expect to rebuild anything that matters, especially auto layout and component overrides.

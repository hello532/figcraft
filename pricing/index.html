Pricing

What free actually costs: FigCraft, Figma, Penpot and Sketch

Four products, four definitions of free — and four different invoices once a team grows past one person. The arithmetic below is the whole point.

Four products, four definitions of free

Free means four things here. FigCraft is free because the source is open and you run the binary. Figma Starter is free because the vendor rents a drafts-only workspace and charges for seats. Penpot Professional is $0 with caps on members, storage and history; Unlimited removes them. Sketch is not free beyond a trial, and its $120 one-time Mac licence excludes collaboration.

Everything below is arithmetic on published list prices checked 2026-10-22, using annual billing. Taxes and add-ons are excluded. The aim is to show where the meter starts running, and to say plainly what each free tier refuses to give you.

Five people, three totals: Figma Full is 5 × $16 = $80 a month, Penpot Unlimited is 5 × $7 = $35, Sketch Standard is 5 × $12 = $60. Five people inside Penpot's eight-member free tier pay nothing, and any number of people on FigCraft pay nothing too. Penpot Unlimited is capped at $175 a month per team, which 25 editors reach at $7 each. Figma charges per seat at every level — twenty Full seats on Organization are $1,100 — and add-ons sit on top, so every figure is a floor. Dev and Collab seats cost $12 and $3 on Professional.

What each free tier quietly excludes

  • Figma Starter: the handoff loopUnlimited drafts make solo work free, but free viewers inspect CSS on paid plans and team features sit behind seats. The boundary is collaboration.
  • Penpot Professional: headcount, storage, timeEight members, 10GB, seven days of history. A bad edit found on day eight is outside the window.
  • Sketch: permanenceThe $120 licence is a year of updates and no collaboration; trial terms were not verified.
  • FigCraft: everything operationalNo hosted service, support contract or team accounts. You supply the machine, updates and backups.

The invoice FigCraft never sends

FigCraft's price is zero and the cost is time. Fonts must sit in a sibling directory named ../craft-fonts (#821), macOS 27 on M1 crashes at launch (#781), interface scale breaks at 1440p (#540, #818), and FreeBSD aarch64 was dropped in v0.10.0. None of that costs money; all of it costs afternoons.

Run it the other way: $16 buys a mature editor with multiplayer, permissions and plugins; $7 buys a browser workspace with team features. FigCraft buys ownership, offline work, an inspectable engine and a CLI and MCP surface past 400 commands, and asks for tolerance of alpha software. Known issues is the honest test.

The honest conclusion

If a team co-edits this quarter, the cheapest serious answer is Penpot Professional at $0 for up to eight members, then $7 per editor with a $175 ceiling. If maturity, plugins and hiring matter more, Figma's $16 seat is expensive but not overpriced. For one designer who never shares a file, $120 once for Sketch is the cheapest option that feels like a purchase.

FigCraft is free in the licence sense and self-run in the operational sense. It is not production-ready for a team: multiplayer is a preview, the public collaboration server is not end-to-end encrypted, and parity is 49% by the project's own estimate. Use it for local, offline, scriptable work on your own disk, and keep a paid seat elsewhere for the collaboration you cannot yet do without.

Questions this page answers

Straight answers, with the limits stated. Every claim here is checkable against the sources linked above.

Is FigCraft really free?

Yes. FigCraft is open source under MIT OR Apache-2.0, runs without an account and sends no telemetry. There is no paid tier to upgrade to. The real cost is operational: you build or download it, manage fonts, take your own backups, and accept early-alpha bugs. The project estimates feature parity at roughly 49%.

What does Penpot cost for a team of five?

Nothing on the Professional tier, which the vendor describes as free and fully featured with 8 team members, unlimited viewers, 10GB of storage and 7 days of version history. Five editors fit inside that. Past eight members, Unlimited is $7 per editor per month, so five editors would be 5 × $7 = $35; the plan is capped at $175 per month per team.

How much does Figma cost for five Full seats?

5 × $16 = $80 per month on Professional with annual billing. The same five seats on Organization are 5 × $55 = $275, and on Enterprise 5 × $90 = $450. Every tier is charged per seat and add-ons are extra, so those totals are a floor. Dev seats are $12 and Collab seats $3 on Professional.

Is there a free version of Sketch?

Not a permanent one that we could verify. Sketch sells Standard at $12, Professional at $24 and Enterprise at $44 per editor per month, billed yearly, plus a $120 one-time Mac-only licence that includes one year of updates and no collaboration. A trial exists but its length and limits were not confirmed from the pricing page we checked.

Do Figma viewers need a paid seat?

Free viewers can inspect and copy CSS on paid plans, so reviewing does not require a Full seat. What the free Starter tier withholds is the team workspace itself: unlimited drafts are free, but shared libraries, team permissions and the dev handoff loop are what the paid seats provide.

What is the hidden cost of FigCraft?

Time, not money. Fonts must live in a sibling directory named ../craft-fonts (issue #821 is open), macOS 27 on M1 has a launch crash (#781), Windows has a DX12 blur problem and 1440p interface scaling is broken (#540, #818). Multiplayer is a preview and the public server is not end-to-end encrypted, so a team still needs another tool.

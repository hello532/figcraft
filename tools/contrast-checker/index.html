Contrast checker

WCAG 2.2 · runs locally · nothing uploaded

Click any suggested colour to copy its hex. The URL updates as you type, so the link you share reproduces the exact pair and text size.

How the ratio is calculated

WCAG 2.2 defines contrast on relative luminance, not on perceived lightness. The arithmetic is short enough to check by hand:

c_linear = c/12.92                     if c <= 0.04045
c_linear = ((c + 0.055)/1.055) ^ 2.4   otherwise     (c = channel / 255)

L = 0.2126·R_linear + 0.7152·G_linear + 0.0722·B_linear

ratio = (L_light + 0.05) / (L_dark + 0.05)          range 1:1 to 21:1

Two consequences worth knowing. First, the ratio is symmetric: swapping text and background gives the same number. Second, the +0.05 means dark colours compress — the difference between #000 and #111 matters far less than between #EEE and #FFF.

Large text is at least 18pt (24px), or 14pt (about 18.66px) when bold. The checkbox above switches which threshold the verdict uses; the individual level pills always show all of them.

Where this fits in a real design file

01

Check the pair, not the swatch

A brand colour can be fine on white and fail on your grey surface. Check the pair you actually ship: text token against surface token, in both themes.

02

Keep the hue when you fix

The suggestion moves lightness only. A blue that fails becomes a darker blue, not a muddy black — which is what usually happens when someone nudges a colour picker at random.

03

Store the pair in variables

In FigCraft, local styles and variables with modes let one name hold light and dark values. The contrast rule then lives in the token pair rather than in a comment.

What it does not do

A ratio is one axis of accessibility, not the whole test.

  • No colour-blindness simulationTwo colours can pass 4.5:1 and still be indistinguishable to a reader with deuteranopia. Pair colour with shape, label or position.
  • No background images or gradientsThe tool compares two flat colours. For text over a photo, check the worst pixel behind the glyphs.
  • Not APCAWCAG 3 is still a draft and its APCA model produces different numbers. This page implements WCAG 2.2, which is what audits currently cite.
  • Not a full auditFocus visibility, target size, reflow and semantics are separate success criteria. This checks 1.4.3, 1.4.6 and the colour half of 1.4.11.

Questions this page answers

Straight answers, with the limits stated. Every claim here is checkable against the sources linked above.

Why does my colour pass in Figma but fail here?

Usually because a different pair is being measured: a text fill against a frame fill, versus the same fill against a semi-transparent surface above it. Blend the transparency into the background colour first, then check the flattened pair.

What does "nearest passing colour" change?

Only HSL lightness. Hue and saturation stay where they were and the tool searches for the smallest lightness move that reaches the required ratio. If no lightness on that hue can pass, no suggestion is offered.

Is 3:1 enough for large text?

For WCAG 2.2 AA, yes: large text needs 3:1. AAA still wants 4.5:1 for large text, and body text needs 4.5:1 (AA) or 7:1 (AAA).

Does this check UI components too?

It reports the 3:1 non-text threshold, which is the colour requirement in 1.4.11. It cannot tell you whether a focus indicator is large enough or whether a control is distinguishable without colour — those need manual checks.

Can I link a specific check?

Yes. The URL carries fg, bg and size, for example /tools/contrast-checker/?fg=5f6368&bg=ffffff&size=body. The Share result button copies the current URL.
